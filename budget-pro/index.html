How this tool thinks
Start from the goal, not the spending.

Most budgets start from expenses and hope. This one starts from the goal. You declare the year's sales number first, and the whole budget arranges itself around it — which flips budgeting from "what did we spend?" to "what does hitting the goal require?"

1 · THE SALES GOAL

Type one number — what you intend to sell this year. Hit Save & recompute and everything downstream recalculates.

2 · THE LINES

Every line gets a behavior. % of sales rides with revenue (COGS at 35% means 35¢ of every dollar sold). Fixed monthly doesn't care how business is going — rent is rent. Manual is for the lumpy stuff you type month by month.

3 · THE GRID

All 12 months, every cell editable. Know December rent goes up? Click the cell and change it — that month sticks, the rest stay computed. The NET PROFIT row at the bottom tells you what the plan actually earns.

4 · THE TARGET

Pick the margin you want — say 13% of a $500,000 goal — and it answers three questions instantly: the target in dollars ($65,000), the spending ceiling that still gets you there ($435,000), and whether this budget as drawn clears it (cushion) or misses it (gap).

5 · THE SANDBOX

Switch to Projected + Sandbox and every line grows a shadow row — a what-if lane. Try "what if payroll were 20% instead of 22?" without touching the real plan. The sandbox keeps its own net.

6 · SNAPSHOTS & IMPORT

📸 Snapshot freezes a locked, dated copy so you can compare January's plan to June's reality. Import P&L history reads a QuickBooks profit & loss export and rebuilds the whole budget scaled to your new goal — last year's truth, stretched to next year's ambition, in one paste.

That's the whole idea: a goal at the top, a behavior on every line, a ceiling on spending — and a green light when the plan actually clears your margin. Export to Excel anytime for a portable copy; your budgets save in this browser automatically.
